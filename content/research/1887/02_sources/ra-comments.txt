[ImNotBatman1602] Mastery #11

This was another game I played on my childhood from that GBA ROM full of NES games. The first time made decent progress, I was confused how the first maze worked and on the second, I got frustrated after running around for seemingly hours in the second-to-last level. But now, I was determined to see this story through and satisfy what my younger self couldn't have achieved.

It's a decent point-and-click adventure game and its puzzles, though sometimes challenging (as there will be moments that you'll be running around screaming "What am I supposed to do?" a lot), but they're not impossible.

I think this game is designed to prevent soft-locks as any item you need can be found before you move from chapter to chapter, so there should be no problem whatsoever to complete this (except at the end where your bag might fill up with 'key items' you cannot drop, thus necessitating a reset).

There are a lot of times where you have to move back and forth between areas to progress the chapter, but once you have completed the game, you'll have already known every story beat from front to back and can speedrun through it as fast as possible for the last achievement (I managed it in about 1 hour and 30 minutes).

Overall, it's quite a nostalgic experience as I remembered drawing out the maps for the mazes on my own and had fun fighting enemies in Finger Wars. Thank you to the developers and achievement makers for this set. I enjoyed it a lot and I hope the rest of you attempting this will do too. 😊

[kaltfront] 35
I regret nothing, everything I did was just, everyone who voted for one of the other AOTW options are the evil ones.

I can't lie and act like it's the peak of gaming. It's a fucking adventure game, with all the pitfalls. But pretty much all old adventure games have those issues - having to repeat interactions on the off-chance something different happens, 16 different action types to use all over the place, etc - at least this doesn't have a cursor for you to try using everything on every part of the screen. Ignoring all those pitfalls, this is mostly a banger. An interesting setting, some funny interactions, unexpectedly unchildish writing while still clearly being a PG game - it's a cool game.
The set has issues though, primarily Full Bag 3 aka Penitentiary Produce. Hopefully it'll be ironed out, but the cheevo is obtainable - just finnicky for now.

Tips - in general, don't be afraid to use a guide. It's still an old adventure game, you'll save yourself a lot of time and nerves. If you're aiming for a mastery, for your first playthrough do not buy any juices in chapter 2, buying them is incompatible with a few cheevos down the line, and you can get everything past it in one playthrough, so once you've beaten the game, you only have to redo chapter 2 for Full Bag 2 aka Saladoria's Cornucopia. In chapter 7 you can help Yam and Asparagus while also getting A Fistful of Lettuce without lucking out with an Octoberry spawn - ignore Spices, for some reason they're not necessary.
The fights that require you to win flawlessly, whether for an achievement or story reasons, all have an abusable pattern of some kind. Especially Minister Pumpkin - losing against him is a game over, meaning you'll have to restart the whole game if you're aiming for the no passwords cheevo, so don't lose. You can find info about those patterns in-game, but if you don't figure it out, the guide also tells you.
Most importantly - have fun. It's a silly game.
